# Persistent Gear

Eclipse Phase resets your equipment every mission because its characters teleport between habitats as data and print new kit on arrival. Nobody in Echo Protocol is doing that. You live in one city, you keep a locker at the Diver's Rest, and the gear you haul out of the ruins is *yours* — at least until the law, the sponsor, or the Syndicate has an opinion. These rules replace the book's "Acquiring Gear" chapter (the GP costs and complexity ratings still apply).

## The Core Rule: Owned vs. Sponsored

Every item you carry is one of two things. Track which.

**Owned gear is yours and persists forever.** You own:

- Your career's Profession Pack from character creation
- Anything bought with your own money (Resources trait, mission pay, m₳)
- Anything acquired by burning a rep favor
- Human-made gear found in the field (story permitting — someone may come looking)
- Artifacts you keep (see Salvage, below)
- Sponsored gear you've bought out

**Sponsored gear is a loan.** Mission GP ([Gear Packs](06-gear-packs.md)) represents your sponsor outfitting the operation. At mission end:

- Expendables used (ammo, sprays, drugs, grenades) are part of the contract — no charge
- Everything else goes back to the quartermaster
- Gear lost or destroyed through recklessness gets docked from pay or dings your rep with the sponsor (GM call; losses in good faith on a hard dive are an expected cost of business)
- **Buy-out:** you may keep any sponsored item by paying its GP value in m₳ (1 GP = 100 m₳; GMs may discount for wear). Sponsors are happy to sell — it's how most divers build their kit

**Unsponsored missions get no GP.** Diving on your own claim, or for a client who pays cash only? You field what you own and what you can buy. This is the strongest reason to maintain your own kit.

### Mission GP Guidance (Replaces the Book's)

| Situation | GP per PC |
|---|---|
| Standard sponsored contract | 10 (one pack, or à la carte) |
| Deep-zone or high-priority sponsored op | 15–20 |
| Rush job, cut-rate client, or probationary crew | 5 |
| Independent dive | 0 — your kit, your problem |

Unspent GP still vanishes at mission start — it's a budget, not currency. The party may pool GP as in the book.

## Salvage

What you find on a dive is the entire point of the diving economy, so it gets real rules.

**Human-made gear** (a dead diver's rifle, an abandoned camp's tools): finders keepers, subject to fiction. Guild custom says a dead member's possessions go to their dive partner — looting an identifiable Guild body and keeping it quiet violates the Code.

**Watcher artifacts are not legally yours.** Under the Santiago Accord and host-nation law, artifacts recovered under license belong to the licensing authority, with mandated distribution percentages. Licensed divers receive the **Diver's Cut: 10% of assessed value, paid in m₳** on declaration, plus the rep that comes from a clean, documented find (+x-rep, and c-rep or r-rep depending on the sponsor).

| Artifact Category | Assessed Value | Typical Diver's Cut |
|---|---|---|
| Scrap | 0.1–1 m₳/kg | beer money |
| Components | 1–100 m₳ | 0.1–10 m₳ each |
| Devices | 100 m₳ – 10 ₳ | 10–1,000 m₳ |
| Data Cores | 10–1,000 ₳ | 1–100 ₳ |
| Unique Items | BTC-only private auction | life-changing, if you live |

Or you can **not declare it.** Fencing an artifact through g-rep gets you 40–60% of assessed value — four to six times the legal cut — and a smuggling felony, a Santiago Accord violation, and a black-market data trail. Forty percent of the artifact trade goes this way. The GM should make both paths viable and neither safe: declared finds attract corporate buyout pressure and rivals; undeclared finds attract inspectors, informants, and the Red Queen's tithe.

**Keeping an artifact** (declared, with a waiver — rare, expensive, usually a sponsor reward — or undeclared and quiet) puts it in your owned kit under the artifact rules below.

## Watcher Artifacts as Gear

Artifact gear doesn't follow human-gear rules:

- **Activation:** requires Resonance, per the interfacing table in [Resonance](03-resonance.md). A Null can benefit from an active artifact but can't turn it on, direct it, or shut it off.
- **No repair, no replication, no blueprints.** Damaged is damaged. Nobody can fix it, copy it, or tell you what half of it does.
- **Every artifact has at least one quirk.** Roll d10 or choose:

| d10 | Quirk |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Keyed** — only works for the first person who activated it |
| 2 | **Loud** — audible hum and whisper-chorus when active; −10 to Infiltrate while in use |
| 3 | **Hungry** — drains conventional batteries within Close range |
| 4 | **Beacon** — lights up resonance sensors; Sensitives feel it in the room |
| 5 | **Intermittent** — on any critical failure while using it, inert for 24 hours |
| 6 | **Moody** — responds to the user's emotional state; ±10 by circumstance, GM's call |
| 7 | **Thermal** — noticeably chills (or warms) its surroundings while active |
| 8 | **Clinging** — +1 additional Exposure whenever activated |
| 9 | **Disruptive** — unshielded electronics within Point-Blank range glitch |
| 10 | **Awake** — it sometimes activates by itself. It's doing something. |

- **Attention:** owning a working artifact is like owning a famous painting that screams. CMC makes buyout offers that escalate in unsettling ways, thieves and Pure Earth treat you as a target, and licensing authorities treat unexplained artifacts as confessions.

## Upkeep & Between-Mission Maintenance

Keep this to one quick step in downtime — it's a checklist, not a minigame:

1. **Mark off expendables** used (and restock: they're owned gear, buy them back at GP cost in m₳, or let your next sponsor cover them).
2. **Repair damaged gear.** Anything that took damage, suffered a critical failure, or got abused: a downtime Hardware Test (appropriate field) plus parts costing half the item's GP value in m₳. Failure means a professional has to do it at full GP value. The book's gear-quality modifiers (−10 to −30 for shoddy/disrepair) are the consequence of skipping this.
3. **Armor that soaked a wound** drops one quality step until repaired.
4. **Storage:** gear not carried lives in your residence or a Guild locker (secure, members only). Stockpiles are story-visible: they can be robbed, seized, or subpoenaed.

**Loadout limits:** use the book's common-sense encumbrance (−10 to −30 when overloaded). The practical cap on hoarding isn't bookkeeping — it's that you can only carry so much down a hundred sublevels, and everything left behind is somewhere.

## Small-Scale Fabrication

The Small Fabber (Techie pack) and workshop tools can produce **Minor-complexity human-made items only**: feedstock costs half the item's m₳ value, timeframe double the book's acquisition time. No ware, no weapons beyond improvised, no artifacts, obviously. General Hives and Protean Swarms don't exist; 2047 fabrication is a good machine shop, not a cornucopia.

## Why This Works (GM Note)

The book warns against gear accumulation taking over. These rules counterweight persistence with: sponsored gear going home, artifact law eating 90% of legal finds, fencing carrying real heat, repair costs, theft/seizure exposure, and carry limits. If a crew still over-accumulates, don't nerf the rules — make the stockpile a plot point. In this setting, *being known to have stuff* is a hook all by itself.
