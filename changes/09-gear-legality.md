# Gear Legality

The core rulebook leaves Restricted [R] gear and the generic License item abstract. These rules make legality concrete: where you can carry what, which license covers it, and how it gets enforced.

## Security Zones

You can classify every location into one of three zones:

- **Unrestricted Zones:** These are places with either a laissez faire attitude toward weapons and armor or limited laws and law enforcement. Personal weaponry and restricted tech is freely allowed as long as they are not used to violate any other laws. No licenses are required here.
- **Restricted Zones:** This covers the vast majority of the government-run world. While the consequences for violating the law vary, restricted gear requires a license of the appropriate level (see License Levels).
- **Prohibited Zones:** These are the high-security government installations, corporate campuses, and highly-restrictive communities. In these areas, no civilian licenses are recognized. Only active-duty corporate security or military personnel are permitted to carry restricted gear.

## License Levels

The core rulebook lists a generic "License" as a Moderate/Restricted/2 GP item that allows a character to perform a restricted activity. We can break this down into three tiers:

### Level 1 License (Personal / Self-Defense)

- **Permits:** All Minor (MIN) Complexity Restricted [R] gear.
- **Examples:** Medium Pistol Firearms, Dermal Toxins, Hacker's Sniffer Apps, or Minor combat augmentations.
- **Cost:** 1 GP (or a Minor favor). Easy to get with a clean background check.

### Level 2 License (Professional / Private Security)

- **Permits:** All Moderate (MOD) Complexity Restricted [R] gear.
- **Examples:** Assault Rifles, Submachine Guns, or Exploit/Spoofer Apps.
- **Cost:** 2 GP (or a Moderate favor). Requires professional justification (e.g., registered bounty hunter, corporate freelancer).

### Level 3 License (Military / Hypercorp Ops)

- **Permits:** Major (MAJ) or Rare Restricted [R] gear.
- **Examples:** Combat Armor, Heavy Weapons, EMP Grenades, or military-grade bots.
- **Cost:** 3 GP (or a Major favor). Rarely issued to private citizens; usually requires the backing of a hypercorp patron or a licensed private military contractor.

## In-Game Enforcement

Enforcement varies from place to place, but here are examples of typical security and enforcement:

- **RFID Verification:** In a high-security setting, automated scanners read the license from a carried or embedded RFID and match that to the RFID signatures of carried gear. Unchipped gear can lead to additional scrutiny.
- **Weapon Scanners:** Many medium-security settings employ weapon scanners at major doorways and transit hubs to detect metal, explosives, propellants, and nanobots. If any of these are detected, the character is expected to provide a valid license.
- **Visual Inspection:** Low-security settings typically rely on visual inspection or third-party reporting to trigger a license check.
- **Fake IDs:** Characters who want to carry restricted gear without jumping through legal hoops can purchase a Fake ID (Maj/R/3). Criminal syndicates will happily encode a Fake ID with a fake Level 2 or Level 3 license to get well-funded folks into places they shouldn't be.

## Zones in the Campaign (GM Reference)

How the default campaign locations map to security zones:

| Location | Zone |
|---|---|
| Ring 0–1 (ECHO-10 outpost, government/military) | Prohibited |
| Ring 2 (corporate) | Prohibited (campus interiors) / Restricted (streets) |
| Rings 3–4 (diving, residential/academic) | Restricted |
| Ring 5 (slums) | Unrestricted — the Syndicate is the only license that matters |
| Gate Hiro / Republic of Polynesia | Restricted, consistently enforced |
| Crater exclusion zones, open ocean, the back roads | Unrestricted |
| Inside the ruins | Nobody is checking. Getting back out with it is the problem |

Two clarifications for how this interacts with the other docs:

- **Licenses are gear** ([Gear Packs](06-gear-packs.md) include L1–L3 Licenses in several packs) and they are **owned**, not sponsored — once acquired, a license persists like any other kit ([Persistent Gear](07-persistent-gear.md)), though authorities can suspend or revoke it in play.
- **Watcher artifacts are not covered by gear licenses.** Artifact possession is governed by salvage law and the Santiago Accord — see Salvage in [Persistent Gear](07-persistent-gear.md). A Level 3 License will not save you from an undeclared data core.
