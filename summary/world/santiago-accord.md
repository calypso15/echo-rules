# The Santiago Accord

## Overview

Signed March 15, 2038 (3 years after First Contact). 147 nations. Foundational governance document for the post-Contact world. Signed weeks after the NATO nuclear strike cover-up collapsed (Indonesian hackers leaked submarine launch footage in early 2038). Replaced discredited Western-dominated institutions. Prevented post-Contact world from descending into outright war.

## Pre-Accord Crisis (2036-2038)

Key crisis vectors:
- Site sovereignty disputes (military forces at 7 damaged sites, competing claims)
- Technology hoarding (60-70% of 2036-2038 artifact harvest stockpiled before sharing obligations)
- Refugee crisis (6M crater-zone displaced; Madagascar diaspora in exile)
- Economic warfare (artifact trade embargoes; competing blocs)
- Institutional collapse (UN Security Council paralyzed; NATO fractured and discredited)

## Santiago Summit

- Location: Santiago, Chile | Duration: 6 months (Sept 2037 - March 2038)
- 159 nations participated | Mediator: UN Secretary-General Elena Vasquez
- Nearly collapsed 17 times over three fault lines:

1. **Accountability**: Crater Coalition demanded war crimes recognition + reparations. US/UK/France refused any language usable in tribunals. Result: Article I uses evasive language; Crater Coalition signed under protest.
2. **Sovereignty vs access**: China refused to recognize India's ECHO-06 control; Antarctic governance contested; Atlantic sites had no EEZ basis. Result: "Host nation" left deliberately undefined for disputed sites.
3. **Technology distribution**: 15% global pool was a compromise (Crater Coalition wanted 40-50%). Outpost Alliance threatened bilateral deals to force multilateral framework. All delegations understood 15% as inadequate but preferable to no accord.

## Key Articles

### Article I: Sovereignty
- Sites belong to host nations; territorial integrity respected; military intervention prohibited
- **Exceptions**: ECHO-02 (Antarctic multilateral authority, 7 claimant states); ECHO-04/11 (Atlantic, nearest-capable-signatory provision — Portugal/Spain, contested); ECHO-06 (India administers, China claims as South Tibet with formal reservation)
- Functions cleanly for 8 of 12 sites; the other 4 rely on political will over settled law

### Article II: Access
- UN observer rights (mandatory presence, quarterly reports)
- Researcher quotas: 15% international slots, merit-based, 2-year max, knowledge sharing required
- Commercial licenses: Auction system, 15% revenue to global pool, safety/insurance mandatory

### Article III: Technology Distribution
- 70% host nation | 15% global development fund | 10% scientific research | 5% humanitarian

### Article IV: Information Sharing
- Monthly discovery reports, safety disclosure, research publication after review
- Mandatory sharing after 3-year exclusivity window

### Article V: Emergency Protocols
- **Site activation**: Immediate notification, evacuation, 72-hour public disclosure ceiling
- **Watcher return**: Emergency Contact Committee (all site-holders + 7 rotating seats); Heritage Foundation protocols take precedence for 90-day contact window; non-aggression pact (legally binding but enforcement against major powers untested)
- **Credibility problem**: The states leading these protocols are the same ones that launched Echo Protocol

### Article VI: Dispute Resolution
- International Court jurisdiction, binding arbitration, sanctions framework, exclusion penalties

## Signatories

- **Full compliance**: Outpost Alliance, Neutral Sciences Union, 89 additional nations
- **Partial**: Crater Coalition (disputed interpretations), China (contentious; disputes ECHO-06 jurisdiction, runs independent reverse-engineering), 22 nations with reservations
- **Non-signatories**: 12 nations (none hold ECHO sites)

## Compliance (2047)

| Area | Rate | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Sovereignty | 95% | 5% exception = ECHO-06 dispute |
| Access | 78% | Australia and US impose security classifications below 15% minimum |
| Technology | 45% | Most consequential failure; Crater Coalition audit estimates 28-32% real figure |
| Information | 62% | ~40% of meaningful discoveries at intact sites never published |
| Emergency | 100% | Never operationally tested; full implementation considered aspirational |

## Major Violations

Australia restricting access, Russia conducting raids, China's reverse-engineering program (largest Article IV non-compliance), corporate circumvention, black market, information hoarding

## Amendments

- **2041**: Medical access, youth protection
- **2044**: Crater site inclusion (gave Coalition legal standing for arbitration); entity encounter procedures (neutral language re: non-human intelligences at deep access levels; Guild provided input); mental health provisions (mandatory screening, minimally enforced)
- **Proposed 2048**: AGI legal status

## Enforcement

- **Bodies**: Santiago Accord Commission, Technical Standards Board, Access Review Committee, Transparency Council, Emergency Response Team
- **Penalties**: Warning > reduced access > license suspension > research exclusion > total isolation (never used)

## Ongoing Disputes

- Artifact percentages (Crater Coalition: 15% inadequate; China: non-holders need more)
- Researcher selection bias favoring allied nations
- Military restriction ambiguity exploited
- 3-year exclusivity window routinely exceeded
- Crater radiation as persistent emergency
- ECHO-06 sovereignty (most intractable)
- Atlantic jurisdiction (ECHO-04/11)
- Reparations (Coalition rejects "humanitarian aid" framing)
