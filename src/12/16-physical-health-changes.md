# Death & Recovery

The single biggest mechanical difference between Eclipse Phase and Echo Protocol: **death is permanent** (at least initially). This document removes the immortality infrastructure as a default assumption and adjusts the rules so that permanent death is dramatic rather than cheap.

## Not Available

The following rules and the technology behind them do not exist in the default starting setting:

- **Backups and uploading** — no cortical stacks, no ego bridges, no backup insurance
- **Resleeving** — no morph acquisition after character creation, no body banks, no Morph Points in play
- **Forking and merging** — there is exactly one of you
- **Egocasting and farcasting** — you travel by plane, boat, and maglev
- **Psychosurgery** — minds are healed by therapy, medication, and time, not editing
- **Identity systems built on ego ID** — identity in 2047 means passports, biometrics, and credentials (all forgeable the old-fashioned way)

Ignore any rule text that references these. The traits, gear, and disorders that depend on them are listed in [Traits](../04/28-traits-changes.md) and the [Prohibited Gear](../16/00-gear-changes.md#prohibited-gear) list.

## Dying (House Rule)

Eclipse Phase can afford to kill characters casually; we cannot. Use the book's damage, wounds, bleeding out, and Death Rating rules with these modifications:

1. **At Death Rating, you are *dying*, not dead.** You are unconscious and beyond self-stabilization. You die after SOM ÷ 5 action turns unless someone stabilizes you with a Medicine: Paramedic Test at −30 (healing spray or meds give the usual +30). A character stabilized from dying takes a permanent reminder: a scar, a lasting injury, or a −5 to a related aptitude check at GM discretion until properly treated in surgery.
2. **Instant death** still happens on unambiguous overkill: damage reaching Death Rating × 1.5, decapitation-grade critical effects, or unprotected exposure to lethal environments. The GM should telegraph these stakes before they're on the table.
3. **Death's Door is once per arc.** The first time in a story arc you'd die, the GM may instead rule you are dying (rule 1) even past the thresholds above. The second time, the dice mean what they say.

This is deliberately a small buffer, not plot armor. Dives are supposed to be frightening; diver life expectancy is 5–7 years.

## When a Character Dies

- The body matters. Recovering a dead diver is a Guild obligation ("Rescue when possible") and the reason Recovery Bonds exist. Death customs: body recovery if possible, memorial at the Diver's Rest, possessions to your dive partner, name on the Wall.
- The player builds a new character at the table's current Rez level. The Guild, a sponsor, or the dead PC's will can justify the new character inheriting specific owned gear — this is the one form of afterlife the setting offers.

## Physical Healing

Natural healing and medical care work per the book, minus the banned tech (no medichines, no healing vats, no nanobandages beyond the allowed list). What you have instead:

- **Healing spray, meds, docbots, and med scanners** (all in the gear packs) for field care
- **Hospitals** for surgery and serious trauma — Nueva Esperanza has three, quality and price follow the ring gradient
- **Downtime**: with at least two weeks of downtime and competent medical care, clear all damage and wounds. With less, heal per the book's natural healing rates. Wounds from a dying state may persist as complications until treated.

Watcher-derived medicine exists at the margins: experimental regeneration treatments are Rare gear, sponsor rewards, or plot hooks — not purchasable kit.

## Aging

People age. Rejuvenation does not exist; the Age negative trait is genuinely permanent, and long-career divers are rare for more reasons than one.
